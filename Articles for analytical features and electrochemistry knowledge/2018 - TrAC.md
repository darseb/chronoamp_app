abstract
Photoelectrochemical (PEC) biosensing is a newly developed and promising analytical technique. The
complete separation of excitation source (light) and detection signal (current) greatly reduces the un-
desired background signal, which is advantageous over both optical and electrochemical determination.
Using a photocurrent from the PEC process as a detection signal, PEC biosensor can be operated at a low
applied potential and exhibits high sensitivity with repeating cycles. In this account, we summary recent
results in the study on PEC biosensing. To construct PEC sensors, exciting light sources of chem-
iluminescence (CL) and electrochemiluminescence, and PEC active materials including the selected
semiconductors, dyes, composites of semiconductors-semiconductors and hybrids of dyes-
semiconductors are employed. The principle of PEC biosensing is described and the mechanism in
anodic and cathodic photocurrent generation processes is well investigated. On the other hand, in typical
PEC biosensors, biomolecules such as antibodies and nucleic acids, are immobilized on the biosensing
interface and bind with their corresponding targets via chemical reaction or biological recognition,
enabling quantitative detection of the targets possible according to the variation of the photocurrents.
Finally, several examples with the PEC biosensing application including immunosensors, DNA sensors,
RNA sensors, aptasensors, enzymatic analysis, cytosensors, and detection of small molecules and metal
ions are brie ﬂy introduced.

1. Introduction
Photoelectrochemistry has attracted considerable attention in
solar cell and water splitting research for two decades [1e3].
Photoelectrochemical (PEC) biosensing is a newly developed and
promising technique in bioanalytical chemistry and photo-
electrochemistry [4e8]. The PEC process refers to the photovoltaic
conversion resulting from the charge separation and transfer of
photoelectrochemically active material in accompanying with the
oxidation-reduction reaction of the ground or excited states of
molecules or ions. In PEC detection, light is utilized to excite the
photoactive species that produces electrons and holes, and then
electrical signal is transduced as the detection readout, which can
be recognized as the reverse of electrochemiluminescence (ECL)
[3e8]. The separation of the excitation source and detection signal
greatly reduces the background signal, resulting in a higher
sensitivity than that in electrochemical or chemiluminescent
techniques [9]. Another exciting light source, chemiluminescence
(CL), is a phenomenon that re ﬂects the transformation of chemical
energy into light emission [10]. ECL involves the generation of
species at electrode surfaces that then undergo electron-transfer
reactions to form excited states that emit light [1 1]. In contrast to
electrochemiluminescent measurements that detect an optical
signal produced from analyte-intervenient electrochemical re-
actions [1 1], PEC biosensing is to detect the photocurrent signal.
PEC biosensing is with advantages over both optical and elec-
trochemical methods [12]. Optical detection methods such as
ﬂuorescence, CL and Raman spectra usually include complex and
expensive optical imaging devices [13e15]. Electrochemical re-
actions usually accomplish at high applied potential or over-
potential [16,17], which may damage biomolecules and cause
interference from other species in the sample during electro-
chemical detection. On the contrary, PEC biosensing can be oper-
ated at a low applied potential with high sensitivity. PEC reactions
are mainly determined by the band structure of the semiconductor
absorbers [18], which may improve the selectivity of PEC

2. Principle of PEC biosensing
2.1. The main components of the PEC biosensor
A PEC biosensor usually consists of a light source, mono-
chromator, electrochemical workstation and signal acquisition
system. Light at the maximum absorption wavelength of the pho-
toelectrochemically active materials is obtained by passing a beam
of light through the monochromator. The monochromatic light
excites photoelectrochemically active materials modi ﬁed on the
electrodes, and application of a bias voltage produces a photocur-
rent that is recorded by the electrochemical workstation. The signal
acquisition system displays the results of the measurement [4e9].
Note that apart from a physical light source, light source for PEC
biosensing can be obtained from CL and ECL systems, which will be
discussed in detail in the next parts.
2.2. Mechanism of anodic photocurrent generation
For composites of semiconductors-semiconductors (n-type),
narrow band-gap semiconductor materials can easily be excited by
visible light, producing electrons and holes in the process [3e8].
Then the transfer of charge from the narrow band-gap materials to
the wide band-gap semiconductor materials can be achieved with
speciﬁc band alignments [19,20]. Electron donors or sacri ﬁcial re-
agents can transfer electrons to the holes in the valence band (VB)
of the narrow band-gap semiconductor materials, thus scavenging
a portion of the photo-generated holes and restraining the
electron-hole recombination of composites of semiconductors-
semiconductors. Finally, photoexcited electrons transfer to the
electrodes such as indium tin oxide (ITO) or ﬂuorine-doped tin
oxide (FTO), generating a stable anodic photocurrent and
completing the photocurrent generation cycle [19] (shown in
Scheme 1 ). Because of their electrical conductivity and optical
transparency, ITO and FTO deposited as a thin ﬁlm are most widely
used in PEC biosensing. The photocurrent is relatively stable over
time and can be turned on and off by controlling the light.
For hybrids of dyes-semiconductors (n-type), the photoinduced
electrons can transfer from excited dyes to the conduction band
(CB) of wide band-gap semiconductor materials when the energy
level of the LUMO of dyes is higher than that of the CB of wide
band-gap semiconductor materials [21e23]. Electron donors or
sacriﬁcial reagents can capture the hole of the excited-state dyes,
restrain electron-hole recombination and inhibit the photo-
corrosion of dyes. During this process, electron donors or sacri ﬁcial
reagents are oxidized. The dyes promote charge separation and
interfacial photoinduced hole transfer in the semiconductor
[21,22]. Finally, the photoinduced electrons transferred to the
electrode, leading to a stable and periodic anodic photocurrent that
was turned on and off by controlling the light to obtain on-off
cycles.
2.3. Mechanism of cathodic photocurrent production
Upon light irradiation, the photoexcited semiconductors (n-
type) undergo electron-hole pair (excitation) generation, which
induces electron and energy transfer processes between the VB and
the CB [13]. For example, at a negatively applied bias, the holes
generated in the VB of CdTe quantum dots (QDs) are scavenged by
electron donors [13]. Simultaneously, together with a proton (H þ)
in Tris-HCl buffered saline, ascorbic acid (AA) serves as the H þ
source for H 2 production [24] (shown in Scheme 2 ). This process
consumes electrons from the CB of the CdTe QDs. In this manner, a
cathodic photocurrent can be periodically turned on and off by
controlling the light, completing on-off cycles [25].
2.4. Principle of PEC detection
PEC biosensing is carried out based on monitoring photocurrent
changes caused by chemical reactions and biological recognition
[4e9]. The biological recognition components include enzymes,
antibodies, nucleic acids, and cells. These components are usually
immobilized on the electrode surfaces. After binding their corre-
sponding targets, the photocurrent variation realizes, which is
recorded for the quanti ﬁcation of the targets (shown in Scheme 3).
In particular, PEC biosensors have promising applications in
monitoring biomolecules that can directly donate electrons to hole-
injected dyes or semiconductors.

3. Exciting light source
3.1. Physical light
Conventional PEC measurement requires a physical light for
excitation, typically ultraviolet and visible light. Larger photocur-
rent can be obtained under the excitation of short-wavelength
light. However, short-wavelength light, such as UV light, may
induce damage of biomaterials, affecting the applicability of PEC
biosensors. Therefore, when physical light is chosen in PEC
biosensor, wavelength of light should be paid special attention.
Under ultraviolet light irradiation, a PEC sensor was fabricated for
detection of protein binding based on gold nanoparticle-decorated
TiO2 nanowire. The direct attachment of gold nanoparticles to TiO 2
nanowires offered strong surface plasmon resonance (SPR) for
electrochemical ﬁeld effect ampli ﬁcation, yielding a ~100% in-
crease of photocurrent density. Cholera toxin subunit B was
robustly detected by the proposed sensor with a high sensitivity
and excellent selectivity [26]. A PEC biosensor was fabricated for
DNA detection based on illumination with 380 nm light and
[(ppy)
2Ir-(dppz)]þ as an intercalated signal reporter. Bene ﬁting
from excellent PEC performance of Ir(III) complex and the ampli-
ﬁcation of the hybridization chain reaction (HCR), the proposed
method exhibited high sensitivity at femtomolar level for DNA
detection [6].
We previously developed a universal signal-on PEC platform
based on resonance energy transfer (RET) between CdTe QDs and
reduced graphene oxide (RGO)-gold nanoparticles (Au NPs)
nanocomposites under visible light (470 nm) irradiation, as shown
in Scheme 2. On the basis of RET and the aptamer as a recognition
module, with the use of carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) as a
model, a PEC aptasensor prepared via DNA hybridization was
constructed [13]. Furthermore, under visible light (405 nm) irra-
diation, a PEC biosensor for miRNA was designed based on DNA-
CdS QDs and single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs)-COOH
(shown in Scheme 4 ). The DNA-labeled CdS QDs were used to
sensitize the SWCNTs-COOH by pep stacking, and thus exhibited
high photovoltaic conversion ef ﬁciency. By integrating cyclic
enzymatic ampli ﬁcation into this bioanalytical approach, this
proposed PEC biosensor achieved sensitive detection of target
miRNA. It was noteworthy that irradiation with visible light
signiﬁcantly reduced the destructive effect of UV light on bio-
molecules [25].
3.2. Chemiluminescence
A physical light source often complicates the instrument and
detection process. A monochromator is occasionally required to
ensure appropriate light emission to excite various photo-
electrochemically active materials. These limits are unfavorable for
the miniaturization of detection devices and in situ detection. CL
emission is a promising substitute for a physical light source.
Adjusting the reaction conditions, CL systems can produce light
emission in different wavelengths to accomplish PEC detection
without a physical light source and monochromator [27].
CL has been used to excite QDs and dyes to generate photo-
current for the read-out of recognition events. A PEC analysis with
CL as a light source to excite CdS QDs for determination of thiols in
cancer cells was proposed [28]. In addition, a catalytic hairpin as-
sembly (CHA)-programmed porphyrin-DNA complex was designed
to trigger the CL as a PEC initiator for DNA sensing. With the cat-
alytic oxidation of iron(III) meso-tetrakis(N-methylpyridinum-4-yl)
porphyrin (FeTMPyP) in luminol-H 2O2-p-iodophenol solution, CL
was generated as the excitation light and subsequently facilitated

QD, which designed a PEC DNA biosensor with CL as light source

3.3. Electrochemiluminescence
In an ECL-induced PEC system, a detection protocol for DNA was
developed based on DNA-linked CdS QDs with methylene blue as
intercalator molecule. Luminol in the ECL cell generated light that
was used as a light source for activation of CdS QDs in an electro-
chemical cell. Therefore, in avoiding of a PEC device, sensitive PEC
detection of DNA was achieved by detecting photocurrent using a
special ECL induced PEC synthesis (The three-electrode PEC system
in the inner cell was placed in the centre of a three-electrode ECL
system in the outer cell that was used to generate light) [31]. The
photocurrent signal was measured by an electrochemistry detector
(CHI 660C electrochemical workstation).
4. Photoelectrochemically active materials for constructing
PEC biosensors
4.1. Semiconductors
Among all the photoelectrochemically active materials applied
in PEC biosensing, semiconductors were the signi ﬁcant candidate.
The use of metal oxide, cadmium or lead-containing QDs and car-
bon materials has been widely reported [5,32e39]. A versatile
“switched-on” PEC biosensing platform was developed based on p-
type PbS QDs. The p-type conductivity property of PbS QDs and
their longer excited-state lifetimes enabled exquisite photoinduced
electron transfer between illuminated PbS QDs and G-quadruplex/
hemin [5]. Using CdS QD-tagged mercury-speciﬁc oligonucleotides,
a folding-based PEC sensor was fabricated for highly sensitive Hg 2þ
detection [33]. Moreover, a PEC immunoassay of HIV-1 p24 antigen
using CdTe QDs combining with poly[G]/S-NPs biological labels was
proposed [32]. ZnO nanorods inorganic-organic heterostructured
light-emitting diodes have been applied in multiplexed PEC
immunosensing [35]. A PEC sensing platform was established by
sequential assembly of near-infrared CdTe quantum dots, capture
DNA, and a hemin-labeled DNA probe. The utilization of CdTe QDs
can extend the absorption edge to the near-infrared band, resulting
in an increase in the light-to-electricity ef ﬁciency [34].
Speciﬁcally, Au NPs were con ﬁned onto the CdS QDs function-
alized PEC surface by the formation of duplex DNA, which
presented a novel energy-transfer-based PEC probing of
DNAeprotein interactions [36]. Direct exciton eplasmon in-
teractions between CdS QDs and Ag NPs were invoked ingeniously
by catalytic Ag deposition on Au NPs for the stimulation of high
efﬁcient damping effect toward the excitonic responses in CdS QDs,
on the basis of which a PEC bioanalytical format was achieved for
sensitive miRNA detection [37]. On the basis of Au NPs deposited on
hierarchically ordered TiO
2 nanotubes, a plasmonic strategy
capable of label-free yet ampli ﬁed PEC immunoassay for the sen-
sitive and speci ﬁc detection of model protein p53 was designed
[38]. Silver nanolabels-assisted ion-exchange reaction with CdTe
QDs-mediated exciton trapping has been developed signal-on PEC
immunoassay of mycotoxins [39]. Based on Au nanocrystal deco-
rated speci ﬁc crystal facets BiVO 4 photoanode, a semiautomated
support PEC immunosensing platform was proposed for portable
and high-throughput Immunoassay [40].
We proposed a robust PEC sensor for Ag þ using enhanced ZnO
nanorod-based photoelectrochemistry by in situ-generated AgBr
via layer-by-layer assembly. Due to their ef ﬁcient absorption of
light, ZnO nanorods are promising for wide-scale adaptation of
solar energy conversion. This strategy of employing in situ-
generated narrow-bandgap semiconductors to enhance the
photocurrent opens up new avenues for PEC biosensing [41].
In another our work, a novel PEC biosensing strategy was
designed for cell assay under 630 nm (red light) excitation. WS

4.3. Composites of semiconductors-semiconductors
Composites of semiconductors-semiconductors have attracted
increasing attention as substitutes for dyes or semiconductors
alone in PEC biosensing. For instance, a PEC platform based on WS2/
TiO2 composites as optoelectronic materials has been designed for
selective detection of o-diphenol and its derivatives. WS 2/TiO2
composites with a band level between WS 2 and TiO 2 facilitate
separation of the photogenerated electron and hole, and thus
signiﬁcantly improve sensitivity [46]. Based on RGO CdS:Mn/ZnS
nanocomposites, a “signal-on” PEC biosensor for sensitive detec-
tion of human T-cell lymphotropic virus type II (HTLV-II) DNA was
fabricated [47]. A three-dimensional mesoporous Fe
2O3-CdS
nanopyramid heterostructure was proposed for the use of selective
PEC biosensing of Cu 2þ in living cells. In this analytical approach,
PEC performance was greatly enhanced, since the Fe 2O3-CdS het-
erostructures facilitated ion transport and provided more active
electrochemical reaction sites [48]. A visible light PEC aptasensor
based on metal-free graphene oxide/water-dispersible graphite-
like carbon nitride nanocomposites for kanamycin detection was
designed [49]. CdS QDs/TiO2 nanoparticles electrode was fabricated
as a new transducer for PEC sensing of cardiac marker (troponin T)
[50]. Recently, TiO2/CdS:Mn hybrid cosensitized with CdTe QDs was
designed to develop an ultrasensitive PEC DNA assay [51]. A truly
economical and valid PEC sensor was established for global anti-
oxidant capacity detection via bicomponent titanium dioxide
decorated sulfonated graphene nanohybrid [52]. Using
Mn:CdS@ZnS core-shell nanoparticles to obtain multi-signal
ampliﬁed photoelectric signals, a PEC sensing platform was fabri-
cated for detecting the activity of caspase-3 [53]. An ultrasensitive
PEC sandwich immunoassay was developed to detect matrix
metalloproteinase-2 based on CdS:Mn/CdTe cosensitized TiO 2
nanotubes (TiO 2-NTs). The TiO 2-NTs/CdS:Mn/CdTe cosensitized
structure could adequately absorb the light energy, dramatically
promote electron transfer, and effectively inhibit the electron-hole
recombination, resulting in signi ﬁcantly enhanced photocurrent
intensity of the sensing electrode [54].
On the basis of the CdS QDs functionalized TiO 2 nanotubes
electrode, a simultaneous PEC immunoassay of dual cardiac
markers using speci ﬁc enzyme tags of alkaline phosphatase (ALP)
and acetylcholine esterase was proposed [55]. An ultrasensitive PEC
immunosensing platform was established on the basis of CdSe-

4.4. Hybrids of dyes-semiconductors
Hybrids of dyes-semiconductors have been reported for enhancing
photocurrent and improving detection sensitivity. Immobilizing a
nanobody on a cobalt 2,9,16,23-te traaminophthalocyanine (CoPc)-
sensitized TiO2 electrode surface enabled creation of a label-free and
highly sensitive PEC immunosensor. The CoPc-sensitized TiO 2 elec-
trode exhibits much higher photocurrent intensity than a pure TiO 2
electrode because the CoPc accel erates the migration of photo-
generated electrons[8]. A triphenylamine-based organic dye (3-5-(4-
diphenylamino-phenyl)-thiophene-2-yl-acrylic acid, TTA) with an
acrylic group was grafted to TiO2 nanoparticles for sensitive and se-
lective PEC detection of small biomolecules. Under irradiation, TTA
exhibits fast intramolecular charge transfer from triphenylamine to
carboxyl group via thep-bridge of thiophene moiety, thus producing a
sensitive photocurrent response[22].
A self-enhanced ultrasensitive PEC biosensor was established
based on a functionalized nanocapsule packaging both donor e
acceptor-type photoactive material and its sensitizer. The func-
tionalized nanocapsule with self-enhanced PEC responses was
achieved ﬁrst by packaging both the donor eacceptor-type photo-
active material (PTB7-Th) and its sensitizer (nano-C 60, fullerene) in
poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) to form a nanocapsule, which signi ﬁ-
cantly enhanced PEC signal and stability of the PEC biosensor [63].
An ultrasensitive “oneoffeon” PEC aptasensor was proposed based
on the signal ampli ﬁcation of a fullerene/CdTe quantum dot (nano-
C60/CdTe QDs) sensitized structure and efﬁcient signal quenching of
nano-C60/CdTe QDs by a manganese porphyrin (MnPP). In the
presence of MnPP, the PEC signal of nano-C 60/CdTe QDs decreased
dramatically and the quenching percentage reached about 82%,
which demonstrated that MnPP could be considered as an ef ﬁcient
quencher of nano-C60/CdTe QDs. Beneﬁting from the efﬁcient signal
quenching of nano-C60/CdTe QDs by MnPP, a low background signal
was obtained to improve the sensitivity [64].

5.5. Enzymatic analysis
PEC biosensing platforms have also attracted considerable
attention in enzymatic analysis. An antileukemia drug evaluation
method was designed based on a multisignal ampliﬁed PEC sensing
platform that monitors the activity of caspase-3, a known marker of
cell apoptosis. Using this sensing platform to detect the activity of
caspase-3, the results indicate that nilotinib can effectively induce
apoptosis of the K562 cells [53]. A LSPR-enhanced and dye-
sensitized turn-on PEC biosensor has been developed for kinase
activity and inhibition assay. The photocurrent generated on the

surface area properties of Au NPs, the signal-to-noise ratio and
sensitivity of the PEC biosensor were greatly enhanced with an
ultralow background. A detection limit of 0.005 U/mL was obtained

excellent performance on quantitative kinase inhibitor screening
and PKA activities detection in complicated cell lysates samples
[43].
A novel PEC biosensor was developed for PKA activity detection
based on combining the PKA-catalyzed phosphorylation reaction in
solution with signal ampli ﬁcation of PAMAM dendrimer and ALP,
which performed a highly sensitive detection for PKA activity with
a low detection limit of 0.048 U/mL (S/N ¼ 3). In addition, the
fabricated PEC biosensor was potentially well for detection of PKA
activity in biological samples and inhibitor screening, which might
be helpful for the discovery of anticancer drugs [60]. Recently, a
general strategy for high-throughput PEC enzymatic bioanalysis on
the basis of enzyme-initiated quinone-chitosan conjugation
chemistry was reported. The methodology had a split-type detec-
tion format without the necessity to immobilize enzymes onto the
electrode, and the mechanism was exempli ﬁed by the combined
use of enzymatic oxidation of the phenolic compounds by tyrosi-
nase and the subsequent speci ﬁc binding of the reaction product to
chitosan on a PbS/NiO/FTO electrode. The enhanced photocurrent
could be achieved by the photoinduced electron transfer from the
PbS QDs to the surface bioconjugated products, making possible the
sensitive probing of activity (with the detection limit of 0.03 U/L)
[84]. In addition, a nanochannels PEC biosensor based on the
integration of a unique Cu
xO-nanopyramid-islands (NPIs) photo-
cathode, an anodic aluminum oxide (AAO) membrane, and alkaline
phosphatase (ALP) catalytic chemistry was presented. The Cu xO-

sembly with AAO yields a designed architecture composed of
vertically aligned, highly ordered nanoarrays on top of the Cu xO-
NPIs ﬁlm. After biocatalytic precipitation was stimulated within the
channels, the biosensor was used for detection of ALP activity with
a low detection limit in a biological ﬂuid [85].

5.8. Determination of metal ions
PEC detection gradually focused on the determination of metal
ions. A PEC sensing platform using 3D mesoporous Fe 2O3-CdS
nanopyramid heterostructure for solar-driven, real-time, and se-
lective detection of Cu 2þ was designed. This promising sensing
platform enables rapid and accurate diagnosis for Cu 2þ with a low
detection limit (~0.5 nM) [48]. Moreover, a PEC sensor based on
ﬂower-like ZnO nanostructures was proposed for Pb 2þ detection,
using a Pb 2þ-dependent DNAzyme as the recognition unit and
Ru(bpy)2(dppz)2þ as the PEC signal reporter. This facile and
economical sensor system showed high sensitivity and selectivity
for Pb 2þ detection, thus can be potentially applied for on-site
monitoring of lead contaminant [97]. Lately, the anodic stripping
method and PEC technique were integrated into a new detection
platform of PEC stripping analysis (PECSA) with bismuth vanadate
as both optoelectronic material and an electrochemical enrichment
candidate. This PECSA strategy also offered new selectivity di-
mensions through the potential-dependent response and thus
implements reproducible, sensitive, and selective detection of Ag þ
in real biological and environmental samples [98].
A cathode PEC strategy was proposed for detection of trace Cu 2þ
based on the formation of analyte-induced exciton trapping that
led to the decrease of cathode photocurrent of mercapto-capped
CdTe QDs. This sensitive cathode PEC method displayed a linear
range from 0.08 to 100 mM for Cu 2þ, which was applied to the
detection of copper in human hair sample [99]. Furthermore, a
wavelength-resolved ratiometric PEC technique was investigated
and employed to construct a new type of PEC sensor for Cu 2þ
detection with good sensitivity and anti-interference ability. This
ratiometric strategy was identi ﬁed to be based on the quenching
effect of Cu 2þ towards CdS QDs and enhancement of the photo-
current through catalytic oxidation of leuco-methylene blue, which
would serve as a novel concept for designing ratiometric or multi-
channel PEC sensors [100].
6. Conclusions and outlook
On the basis of several selective PEC architectures, PEC bio-
sensors are successfully fabricated. The design, mechanism and
performance of the sensors are well described. It is clear that the
application of PEC biosensing has opened a new way in bio-
analytical and bioelectroanalytical chemistry to fabricate immu-
nosensor, DNA sensor, RNA sensor, aptasensor, cytosensor and
biosensors for small molecules and metal ions, although the pre-
sent successes usually appeared in laboratory. The further study in
PEC biosensing will focus on: (1) Effective materials. For instance,
long-wavelength excited materials are attractive in the assays of
real samples. The preparation of such desirable materials and the

believed that with the continuous efforts, PEC biosensing may

and bioanalytical chemistry.
