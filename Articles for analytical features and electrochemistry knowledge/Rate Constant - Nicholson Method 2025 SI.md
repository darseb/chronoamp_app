## **Experimental Section** 

All chemicals and reagents employed in this research were of analytical grade and utilized as received, without further purification. Solutions were formulated using doubly distilled Milli-Q water, processed through a Millipore water purification system with a resistivity of 18 MΩ cm. Solid Ru(NH3)6 Cl3 , K3[Fe(CN)6] and Eu(NO3)3.5H2O were individually dissolved in 1 M KCl to create solutions of [ Ru(NH3)6][3+] , [Fe(CN)6][3―] , and Fe[3+] , respectively. The electrochemical studies were performed using a CHI 760D electrochemical workstation within a 20 ml electrochemical cell, which contained 10 ml of electrolyte solution, with or without the analyte species present. The standard three-electrode cell configuration included a platinum (Pt) disk (diameter, φ = 2 mm) or a gold (Au) disk (φ = 2 mm) as the working electrode, a Pt wire gauze as the counter electrode, and an Ag/AgCl/3 M KCl reference electrode ( E[0] f[ = 0.210 V vs. NHE) used for voltammetric measurements. Before their use, the Pt and Au ] disk electrodes underwent polishing with aluminum oxide (Al2O3) powder (0.5 µm), were thoroughly rinsed with Milli-Q water, and were sonicated in Milli-Q water for one minute. The electrodes were then electrochemically cleaned through potential cycling in a 1 M H2SO4 solution solution, covering a potential range from 1.5 to -0.5 V over 25 cycles. Following this, cyclic voltammetry (CV) was conducted in the same acidic environment, employing a potential range of 1 to -0.2 V until a stable baseline response was obtained. Prior to initiating any electrochemical experiments, high-purity nitrogen (N2) gas was bubbled through the solutions for 10 minutes, and the N2 flow was sustained above the solution throughout the experiments. The potential values reported are referenced to the Ag/AgCl/3 M KCl electrode, unless stated otherwise. All CVs included in this manuscript have been corrected for background signal and adjusted for ohmic drop, unless otherwise specified (further details can be found on pages S5-S7). The initial scan is conducted in the cathodic direction, with a stabilization period of 10 seconds at the starting potential, and the CVs are recorded for a single cycle. After obtaining a CV in a fresh electrolyte solution, the solution was magnetically stirred for 30 seconds to refresh the vicinity of the electrode surface. Once stirring was halted, the solution was permitted to settle to reduce convection effects prior to capturing the subsequent CV at varying scan rates.[1, 2]

## **Ohmic Drop Compensation** 

Ohmic drop compensation is executed through a series of steps, specifically outlined for the [ Ru(NH3)6][3+] /[Ru(NH3)6][2+] redox couple. A 20 ml electrochemical cell was utilized, incorporating 10 ml of a 1 M KCl electrolyte within a conventional three-electrode setup. This configuration consists of a polished platinum working electrode (φ = 2 mm), a platinum wire gauze counter electrode, and an Ag/AgCl/3 M KCl reference electrode. The electrochemical cell was subsequently connected to a CHI760D potentiostat using suitable electrochemical leads. The solution was purged with argon gas for 10 minutes, ensuring that the argon flow remained above the solution throughout the experiment. Following this, the iR compensation setting in the potentiostat software was disabled. The 1 M KCl solution was then substituted with 4.9 mM [Ru(NH3)6][3+] in 1 M KCl. Initially, the resistance ( Ru ) of the solution was assessed using CHI760D software, revealing Ru = 18 Ω. Subsequently, the iR compensation feature within the software was activated, and the measured Ru value was entered. A cyclic voltammetry (CV) experiment was then performed using this Ru value. Should oscillations appear in the current-time response, the Ru value was reduced; conversely, if no oscillations were detected, the Ru value was increased until the maximum Ru value was identified that did not induce oscillations, which would represent the true Ru of the solution. In this instance, it was determined to be 20 Ω for the [ Ru(NH3)6][3+] /[Ru(NH3)6][2+] redox couple at a scan rate of 1000 V/s, as illustrated in Figure S3.

## **Background Subtraction** 

S6 

Background subtraction is executed through a series of steps, specifically outlined for the [Ru(NH3)6][3+] /[Ru(NH3)6][2+] redox couple. Initially, the iR compensation was conducted as previously detailed. Subsequently, the CV of the blank solution (1 M KCl) was recorded at different scan rates for a single cycle, which is referred to as background voltammograms. Following this, the CV experiment utilizing 10 ml of 4.9 mM [Ru(NH3)6][3+] in 1 M KCl solution was repeated within the same electrochemical window. The CVs of [Ru(NH3)6][3+] are adjusted through the background subtraction method. This process entails subtracting the CVs of the blank solution from those of [Ru(NH3)6][3+] , achieved using the potentiostat software CHI760D.[1] The background subtraction methodology is illustrated in Figure S4 for a single scan rate of 1000 V/s. The CVs have not undergone baseline correction, and such correction is not applied during the experiments unless explicitly mentioned.
