ABSTRACT:
 Revolutionary developments in analytical chemistry have led to
the rapid development of self-powered photoelectrochemical (PEC) sensors.
Different from conventional PEC sensors, self-powered PEC sensors do not
require an external power source or complex devices for the sensitive detection
of targets. As a result, these sensors have enormous application potential for
the development of novel portable sensors. An increasing body of work is
making excellent progress toward the implementation of self-powered PEC
sensors for detection, but there have been no reviews to date. The present
review first introduces the state of the art in the development of self-powered
PEC sensors. Then, different types of self-powered PEC sensors are
summarized and discussed in detail, including their current, power, and
potential. Additionally, single- and dual-photoelectrode systems are classified
and systematically compared. Finally, the current developments and major
challenges that need to be addressed are also summarized. This review provides valuable insights into the current state of self-
powered PEC sensors to promote further progress in this field.

hotoelectrochemical
 (PEC) analysis is now regarded as
one of the most promising approaches for ultrasensitive
detection applications, because of its high sensitivity, fast
response, and easy miniaturization. PEC analysis applications
have been rapidly developed in recent years for disease
diagnosis, medical research, food safety, and environmental
monitoring.1
,2During
 PEC detection, a photoactive material
converts optical energy into electrical energy when the
photoelectrode surface of the PEC sensor is illuminated, and
a transducer converts biochemical information from the
analyte concentration in the sample into an electrical signal.3
,4Therefore,
 photoactive materials play an essential role in PEC
sensors. In previous studies, a bias potential was applied during
the sensing process to inhibit photogenerated electron−hole
pair recombination, accelerate carrier transfer, and enhance the
signal at the photoactive sensing interface, thereby improving
detection sensitivity. However, the bias potential can impose
undesired background noise and lead to unreal results, while
relatively high voltages can damage the electrode surface.
In contrast to conventional PEC sensors, self-powered PEC
sensors, without an external potential, allow chemical reactions
to occur only when the optical drive is active (eliminating the
need for an external power supply). This provides the energy
for the detection of various analytes, avoids interference at the
sensing interface, reduces background signals, and avoids false
positives.5
These
 sensors have been widely focused on because
of their many advantages, such as simple instrumentation,
rapid response capability, superior sensing performance, and
environmental friendliness (only consuming solar energy).6
Additionally,
 these self-powered PEC sensors hold promise for
the future development of miniaturized and lightweight sensors
or portable detection.7

CURRENT
 AS THE DETECTION SIGNALThe
 first self-powered PEC sensors were similar to conven-
tional PEC sensors; that is, they detected the change in the
photocurrent to quantify the analyte. In this review, current-
type self-powered PEC sensors are divided into two main
categories: single-photoelectrode- and dual-photoelectrode-
based sensors.
Single Photoelectrode System. Traditional Three-
Electrode System. The original self-powered PEC sensors
used a conventional three-electrode system: an electrode
modified with photosensitive material as the working electrode,
a platinum (Pt) electrode as the counter electrode, and a Ag/
AgCl electrode as the reference electrode (Figure 2A-a). As the
surface of the photosensitive material is illuminated, the
photogenerated electron−hole separation induces the direc-
tional transfer of the carriers, achieving the quantitative
analysis of the target by the PEC sensor. Therefore, the
photosensitive material is a key component in PEC sensors.
Compared with traditional PEC sensors, self-powered PEC
sensors have greater demands for the selection of photo-
sensitive materials. Usually, the photosensitive materials in
these sensors have superior photoelectric conversion efficiency,
such as their light absorption rate, charge separation rate, and
charge mobility, so sensitive detection can be achieved without
an additional voltage drive, effectively eliminating possible
interferences from other substances.14
Since
 the photocurrent
output is closely related to the photoactivity of the photo-
electrode material, improving the photoactivity of the photo-
electrode is a hot research topic for this type of self-powered
PEC sensor. In this section, the photoanode and photocathode
are discussed separately, based on the main carrier type.13
Generally,

photosensitive materials for photoanodes. These n-type
semiconductor materials, with electrons as the major charge
carriers, have received enormous attention because of their
wide variety, ease of synthesis, and ease of modification.15
,16These

high sensitivity for fabricated self-powered PEC sensors. For
example, ZnO has been widely studied, because of its high
electron mobility. However, its wide band gap means that ZnO
absorbs only a small fraction of sunlight in the UV range. To
address this issue, Zhao et al.17
decorated

separation and transfer process. At zero bias potential, this
sensor had a large photocurrent and a satisfactory detection

photoanode is based on an n-type semiconductor material and
provides the sensor with a stable photocurrent as the sensing
signal by generating numerous photogenerated electrons. The
photocathode is based on a p-type semiconductor material as
the working electrode and achieves the detection of the target
by fixing the recognition element.38

photoelectrons. Ordinarily, photoanode materials need to

from the photoanode, effectively inhibiting electron−hole pair
recombination in the system.23

CONCLUSIONS
 AND PERSPECTIVESWith
 the development of science and technology, various self-
powered PEC sensors have been proposed. As an emerging
research topic, there has been increasing research and great
progress on self-powered PEC sensors in recent years. In this
review, self-powered PEC sensors were classified according to
different output signals. First, self-powered PEC sensors with
current as the output signal were summarized and exemplified.
Second, avoiding the complexity of detection equipment were
self-powered PEC sensors with power as the output signal.
Finally, combining 3D printing and microfluidics was self-
powered PEC sensors with an OCP as the output and the
introduction of a digital multimeter. This review focuses on
single- and dual-photoelectrode systems in depth. These self-
powered PEC sensors with excellent material properties enable
the sensitive detection of targets without external power. To
further decrease the interference caused by matrix effects, a
carbon-based electrode immobilized recognition element was
used to separate it from the photoelectrode to provide the
sensor with better anti-interference properties. Based on the
high anti-interference capability of the sensor, dual-photo-
electrode systems were introduced. These benefit from the
Fermi energy level difference between the photoelectrodes,
facilitating the ultrafast transfer of electrons and the efficient
inhibition of charge recombination in the system, resulting in a
higher sensitivity of the sensor. The aim was to summarize the
different types of self-powered PEC sensors for a wide
readership, thereby promoting the development of self-
powered PEC sensing.
Despite self-powered PEC sensors having flourished in
recent years, they are still in the preliminary stage, because of
their relatively short history of development. Current photo-
sensitive materials are relatively primitive and complicated to
prepare and have high requirements for light sources, which
makes it difficult for them to display excellent performance in
on-site detection and miniaturized applications. Therefore,
there is an urgent need to develop appropriate semiconducting
species with new properties that allow the generation of
improved and stable photocurrents for specific aims.
A variety of semiconductor materials with excellent proper-
ties have been developed (Figure 5). Among these, single-atom
materials play an important role in photocatalysis and
photoelectrochemical water decomposition due to their high
activity, excellent durability, etc.64
,65Similarly,
 perovskite is of
interest due to its extremely high photoelectric conversion
efficiency, but its instability makes it difficult to apply in
sensing. Additionally, graphyne-based materials with good
electrical conductivity and excellent chemical stability exhibit
good prospects for photoelectrochemical analyses in the
future.66
Some
 mature semiconductors have also been
modified to prepare materials with a superior performance.
For example, the visible light activity of TiO2
�
a semi-
conductor with high electron mobility and excellent chemical
stability that can usually only be excited under ultraviolet
light�can be enhanced by metal or nonmetal doping, narrow
bandgap semiconductors, metal nanoparticles, and other
materials.67
The
 properties of semiconductor materials can
also be improved by atom doping. For example, the
introduction of S atoms into the MXene skeleton can increase
its interlayer spacing and improve its electrical conductivity
compared with undoped MXene, which helps to accelerate
electron transfer and improve the PEC signal.68
Further
advancements in materials and analytical techniques will
provide great opportunities for the further development of
self-powered PEC sensing technologies, broadening their
application prospects.
Dual-photoelectrode PEC self-powered sensors can signifi-
cantly amplify the sensing signal and achieve sensitive
detection without an external power source. Existing studies
indicate that the mechanism of signal amplification is related to
the Fermi level difference between the two electrodes, the
electrical conductivity of the photocathode substrate, etc.69
However,
 the detailed operating mechanism of these systems is
still obscure; therefore, further investigation is crucial to
establishing successful self-powered on-site detection. For
portable on-site detection, an emerging approach is to
introduce flexible electrodes�such as paper-based electrodes,
laser-induced graphene electrodes, etc.�in combination with
microfluidic technology, and this could expand the advantages
of self-powered PEC sensors.70
Self-powered
 PEC sensors will
likely play key roles in daily life in the future.■
