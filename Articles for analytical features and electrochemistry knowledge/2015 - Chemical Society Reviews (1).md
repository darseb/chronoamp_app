Photoelectrochemical bioanalysis: the state of
the art
Wei-Wei Zhao, Jing-Juan Xu* and Hong-Yuan Chen*
The evolution of photoelectrochemical (PEC) bioanalysis has resulted in substantial progress in its
analytical performance and biodetection applications. The aim of this review is to provide a panoramic
snapshot of the state of the art in this dynamically developing field, with special emphasis on PEC DNA
analysis, immunoassay, enzymatic biosensing and cell-related detection. The future prospects in this
area are also evaluated and discussed. This work will serve as a useful source to inform the interested
audience of the latest developments and applications in the field of PEC bioanalysis.
1. Introduction
The photoelectrochemical (PEC) process refers to the photo-to-
electric conversion resulting from electron excitation and subse-
quent charge transfer of a photoexcited material produced by an
applied light.
1–10 Electrochemical bioanalysis has been increas-
ingly important and has generated a huge impact on our lives.11–20
As well as the prompt development of nanotechnology and
material science, the marriage of the PEC process to electro-
chemical bioanalysis creates real opportunities to advance PEC bio-
analysis, which provides an elegant route for probing various
biological interactions.
21–27 Essentially, like other well estab-
lished analytical techniques such as electrochemiluminescence
(ECL), the PEC technique is also the evolutionary generation of
the electrochemical method.
28–30 It naturally inherits the
advantages of the latter such as low cost, simple instrumenta-
tion and high sensitivity. However, a great difference exists
between them and the PEC technique offers some merits that
could not be accomplished on the conventional platform. In
PEC detection, light is utilized to excite the photoactive species
and the electrical signal is transduced as the detection readout,

2. Principle, classification, and
mechanism
2.1 Principle
Today, numerous configurations for PEC bioanalysis have been
developed towards various analytes of interest. In a typical one,
however, two critical ingredients are essential: the PEC active
species (to generate the detection signal), and the biological
recognition elements (which usually are in intimate spatial
contact with the transducer). Like in many other bioanalysis
techniques, the biological recognition components include
enzymes, antibodies, nucleic acids, etc. As for the PEC active
species, organic/inorganic semiconductors are used in most
cases, such as the tris(bipyridyl) ruthenium complex
[Ru(bpy)
3]2+ (bpy = 2,2 0-bipyridine) and CdS quantum dots
(QDs).21–25 For instance, Long et al. have reported the novel
use of Nafion-functionalized CdS QDs to realize the ultrasensitive
determination of cysteine. 28 However, it is worth noting that
some biomacromolecules such as DNA and cells can generate
photocurrents by themselves. 43,44 The principle of the PEC
bioanalysis is based on monitoring the biological interactions
between various recognition elements and their corresponding
targets via current/voltage with an array of electrodes.
Specifically, the biological recognition systems transfer the
biochemical information ( e.g. an analyte concentration) into a
variation in the specific factor on or surrounding the electro-
des, the change of which is associated with the PEC active
species. When under illumination, the transducer would
subsequently convert the physical–chemical interactions
between the biological recognition systems and PEC active
species to the electrical signal. General instrumentation and
the working principle of PEC bioanalysis is schematically
shown in Fig. 2.

2.2 Classification
Bioanalysis may be classified according to diﬀerent criteria. In
terms of the biological specificity-conferring mechanism, it
may be divided into two main categories: biocatalytic devices
and aﬃnity sensors. Biocatalytic devices incorporate biological
components such as enzymes that recognize their specific
substrate molecules. Aﬃnity sensors involve the strong and
selective binding interactions between a recognition biomolecule
and analyte, such as antibody–antigen (Ab–Ag) immunocomplexa-
tion and DNA hybridization. Alternatively, according to the
mode of signal transduction, the PEC bioanalysis could also
be organized into two modes: potentiometric
45 and ampero-
metric.21 The potential type mainly refers to the LAPS, a kind of
semiconductor device based on the surface photovoltage
technique, which was proposed originally in 1988 by Hafeman
et al.46 Since then, the LAPS has undergone in depth exploitation
for various analytical applications concerning ions,47,48 DNA,49–52
immunomolecules,53–56 enzymes (substrates and inhibitors)56–61
and cells,62–64 and a survey of the LAPS can be found in an earlier
comprehensive review.65 The current mode relies on directly
measuring the photocurrent as the analytical signal, which
commonly results from the response of the transducer to the
biological binding event. As we discuss later, this mode has
been developed extensively in the past decade and still
continues to progress. Ideally, the potential/current is proportional
to the concentration of analyte in the sample, on the basis of
which the quantitative or semi-quantitative determination can be
performed. In fact, PEC bioanalysis may further be sorted with
respect to the analytes or reactions that it monitors: direct
monitoring of analyte concentration or monitoring of reactions
producing or consuming such analytes, or indirect monitoring of
the inhibitor or activator of the biological recognition element.
11

3. Bioanalytical applications
PEC bioanalysis has become an invaluable tool for the specific
and sensitive detection of manifold biochemical species of
clinical and environmental significance. On the basis of the
chosen biorecognition elements aiming at diﬀerent targets,
various PEC bioanalyses have been proposed for particular
analytical purposes. A model of these works may be demonstrated
by the simplest protocol of biotin–avidin recognition.
31,69,70 Here,
to reflect the recent advances in PEC bioanalysis, the discussion in
this section will centre on four main aspects of bioanalytical
applications: DNA analysis,
71–110 immunoassay,111–140 enzy-
matic sensing 141–172 and cell-related detection, 67,105,167,173–175
as detailed below.
3.1 DNA analysis
Among the diverse DNA detection platforms, the PEC technique
has received considerable interest due to its versatility, simple
experimental set-up, etc. On the other hand, of all the PEC
biosensing subjects, DNA analysis has been the most extensively
investigated. The popularity of this research hotspot, in our view,
should be attributed to the interesting combination of the
following several factors: the potential of PEC method, the
importance of DNA, as well as the peculiar properties of DNA.

bioanalysis. A vast majority of recent works has been founded

3.2 Immunoassay
Due to its great potential in biomedical applications, PEC
immunoassay has also gained momentum in recent years.
Compared with the manifold protocols of PEC DNA analysis,
recent PEC immunoassay systems are comparatively simple
because of the insulating features of (Ag and/or Ab) proteins.
They generally necessitate the immediate use of the PEC
transducer for the subsequent association of probe proteins
onto its surface. In such a configuration, taking advantage of
the PEC transducers, the PEC immunoassay system would then
convert the specific immunorecognition events into electrical
responses for probing various immunoreactions.
PEC immunosensors are convenient biosensing devices
based on the coupling of immunochemical reactions to appropriate
PEC transducers. To date, the functions of established PEC
immunosensors are accomplished by two general mechanisms:
(1) steric hindrance produced from the formation of immuno-
complexes, and (2) altered signal generation caused by an
enzymatic reaction. The first mechanism lays down the
common foundation for the reported label-free PEC immuno-
sensors. Nonetheless, despite a good level of simplicity, the
analytical properties of the label-free protocols still need to be
improved due to poor capacities for analyte loading and inability
for amplification. Under such a background, enzyme-based PEC
immunoassays, including the popular formats of sandwich and
competitive, have been developed since it is a feasible route for
signal enhancement. As for the potential enzyme labels, the
usual candidates are alkaline phosphatase (ALP),b-galactosidase
(b-Gal), horseradish peroxidase (HRP), and glucose oxidase (GOx).
With a lower activity than the other enzyme tracers, GOx is
typically used in amperometric immunoassays where the product
is detected directly.

4. Conclusions and perspectives
Recent years have witnessed tremendous advances towards the
development of PEC bioanalysis. Such methodologies are of
considerable interest due to their innovative routes for biomo-
lecular detection. Of all the PEC bioanalysis research, the most
active directions have been and will continue to be PEC DNA
analysis, PEC immunoassay and PEC enzymatic sensing. In
particular, PEC DNA analysis is the most rapidly expanding
area, as evidenced by the vast quantity of recent publications.
While significant eﬀort was previously directed toward DNA
hybridization/damage, the focus on RNA
177,178 and other DNA
related analytes ( e.g. drugs and proteins) continues to drive its
development.
In comparing the sensing strategies, the introduction of PEC
labels/indicators and producing electron donors/acceptors
and/or steric hindrance are among the simplest approaches
available. However, there is still room for developing a new
detection format based on novel principles. For example, one
recent trend has been the use of exciton–plasmon interactions
between QDs and noble metal nanoparticles in the PEC system
for biosensor applications.
92,93 Incidentally, some previously
established protocols in other PEC-related fields may also be
adapted for biosensing purposes. 179 Another major trend is
associated with the design and improvement of PEC biosensing
equipment. The common PEC detection methods suffer from
the drawbacks of bulky and delicate light source instrumentation,
which means that the usual PEC detection necessitates the use of
physical light for the excitation of the PEC species. This is
circumvented by using the luminescence generated from the
chemiluminescence reaction to trigger photocurrent generation
of PEC species in the biosensing system.
85,180
Although the desirable merits in current PEC bioanalysis are
obvious, some important challenges and hurdles still remain.
For example, only a few photoactive materials are exploited in
established formats. Specifically, previous works rely largely on
the TiO
2 and QD-based materials, which are often subject to
severe problems such as relatively low quantum yields/photon-
to-current conversion eﬃciencies and susceptibilities to
photobleaching. Proper semiconductive species with novel
properties that allow the generation of improved and stable
photocurrents are thus needed to meet the criteria for specific
aims. Furthermore, the way in which the recognition event is
integrated into the PEC system is critical for the establishment
of a successful bioanalysis. In this regard, the impact on
surface-confined biological molecules must be carefully
controlled. In particular, the real application value of the
proposed analysis depends intimately on the analytical
performance. However, in most cases the existence of non-
specific adsorption and nonspecific oxidation/reduction of
interferants remain hurdles for the implementation of these
biosensors in practical and industrial analysis. The resulting
low reproducibility and selectivity have been the major
barricades that prevent their further application. Future
research should be directed towards solving these problems.
Tremendous opportunities also exist in prospective bio-
sensing systems. Coupling newly developed PEC active materials
with other recognition elements such as protein receptors and
tissues would open new areas in PEC bioanalysis research and
extend the PEC biosensors’ applications. Particular attention
should be given to photo-induced electron-transfer in tailored
biomolecule–semiconductor hybrid systems and their novel
uses in bioanalytical applications, such as using the photo-
excited electron–hole pair for in vitro probing of intracellular
processes or in vivo analysis. The use of photo-induced electron-
transfer of some special photosensitive proteins may establish
an exciting new field with bright perspectives.
181 As efficient
biosensing elements, the photosynthetic photosystems have
enormous potential in future PEC bioanalysis.
182 There is
great room for engineering various photosystems toward
numerous targets of interest. Integration into flow-injection
systems and (paper-based) microfluidic platforms 131,132,140,183
should lead to significant advantages compared to the
current bioanalysis, in terms of simplicity, speediness, cost
and automation. Improvement in analytical techniques
with differences in time, space, labels and so on will lead to
multi-analyte biosensing, and progress in the instrumentation
such as using scanned light and electrode arrays may, on the
other hand, result in the simultaneous detection of several
samples. Screen-printed and advanced microtechnological
approaches will also benefit the manufacture of PEC bio-
sensors.
41,182 With its rapid progress in recent years, we believe
that PEC bioanalysis will certainly play a bigger role in future
biosensing.
